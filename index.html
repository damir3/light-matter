Outer Valence Shells & Dispersion Role

The optical dispersion of matter in optical, UV, and IR frequencies is dictated by the response of outer valence electrons to the oscillating electric field of light waves:

1. Complex Permittivity ε̃ and Refractive Index ñ = n + ik
ε̃(ω) = ε₁ + iε₂ = (n + ik)² = (n² - k²) + i(2nk)

Inverting the relationship yields exact analytical equations for n and k:
n = √[ (|ε̃| + ε₁) / 2 ]
k = √[ (|ε̃| - ε₁) / 2 ], where |ε̃| = √(ε₁² + ε₂²).

2. Free Electrons and Core Polarization (Drude Model)
ε_Drude(ω) = ε_∞ - ω_p² / [ ω(ω + iγ₀) ]

Delocalized outer electrons (e.g. 6s¹ in Au, 5s¹ in Ag) form a plasma with frequency ω_p. Screening from filled d-shells provides high background permittivity ε_∞ ≈ 5.9 (Au) and ε_∞ ≈ 3.7 (Ag).

3. Bound Electrons & Interband Transitions (Lorentz Oscillators)
ε_Lorentz(E) = ∑ [ S_j ] / [ (E₀,j² - E²) - i·E·Γ_j ]

Describes dipole transitions between atomic orbitals (e.g. 5d → 6s at the L-point near 2.72 eV and 5d → 6p at the X-point near 3.65 eV in gold).

4. Optical Reflectance and Attenuation
R = [ (n-1)² + k² ] / [ (n+1)² + k² ]

Bouguer absorption coefficient: α(λ) = 4πk / λ (cm⁻¹).
Optical penetration / skin depth: δ = λ / (4πk).
